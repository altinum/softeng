In the next lab session, we will use microsimulation to estimate how many people will live in Hungary fifty years from now. For the projection, we have access to census data, as well as mortality and fertility data collected by the Hungarian Central Statistical Office.

- Mortality tables show the probability that a person of a given age and sex will die within a calendar year.
- Fertility tables show the probability that a woman of a given age will have a child. The table also takes into account the number of children the woman already has, since a fourth child is less likely to be born than a second child.

In the microsimulation, we will determine the fate of ten million Hungarian citizens in annual simulation steps, using the tables and a random number generator. Since the simulation is fairly computationally intensive, we will also gain some practical experience of what an average CPU is capable of.

During the practical session, we will also look at how the simulation can be parallelized so that we can make use of all available CPU cores.
